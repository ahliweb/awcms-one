Placement follows [ADR-0040](adr/0040-aw-business-platform-capability-ownership-and-boundaries.md). Written 10 October 2026.

> **Not legal advice, and no compliance claim.** This document is an engineering input. Sections 6 and 7 list regulations and standards that a qualified Indonesian counsel and the deployment's owner must assess for the real deployment and business model. Nothing here states that any deployment complies with, or is certified against, any law or standard. **Nothing described in this document is implemented**: it is a design-time analysis of adapters that do not exist yet, and every control status below is "planned". ADR-0040 D7 forbids a module, migration, OpenAPI path or DDL until the Definition of Ready is met; this document adds none.

## 3. Method, assets, actors and flows

**Method.** STRIDE per flow (Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service, Elevation of privilege). A row is a threat, the planned mitigation, and its basis: **precedent** (a mechanism that already runs in `commerce` today and would be reused), **upstream** (a property the upstream module is specified to provide), or **new** (a requirement the future adapter must meet). "Precedent" describes the existing order/payment surfaces; it does not claim the booking surfaces have it.

**Assets.** (a) Money integrity: the payment-allocation ledger, refund legs, loyalty and stored-value ledgers. (b) Reservation integrity: a confirmed reservation corresponds to a real, paid-or-policy-permitted claim. (c) Personal data of customers and of tenant employees. (d) Provider credentials (Midtrans server key, WhatsApp tokens, a future Telegram bot token). (e) Tenant isolation.

**Actors.** Anonymous public caller; signed-in customer (bearer session, ADR-0016); staff with commerce permissions; payment provider (Midtrans) calling back; messaging providers; domain-event dispatcher (a system actor); an attacker who knows or guesses identifiers.

**Flows.**

| ID  | Flow                                                                                              | Direction and parties                                                    |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| F1  | Public booking endpoints (availability, hold, confirm intent, view/cancel own reservation)        | Browser to CMS, anonymous or customer bearer                             |
| F2  | Deposit and payment observation, including provider webhook and reconcile into the payment ledger | Midtrans to CMS (inbound), CMS to Midtrans (session create, status poll) |
| F3  | Cancellation and refund coordination                                                              | Booking event to commerce return/refund records, to provider refund API  |
| F4  | Reminders and notifications (WhatsApp now, e-mail, push; Telegram per O10; orchestration per O11) | Domain event to outbox to provider                                       |
| F5  | Commission accrual source events                                                                  | Commerce/Booking event to upstream commission port                       |
| F6  | Provider boundaries (credentials, timeouts, breakers, no I/O in a transaction)                    | CMS to and from every external service                                   |
| F7  | Domain-event consumers that implement F1 to F5 and the loyalty earn from a reservation            | Dispatcher to adapter consumers                                          |
| F8  | CRM segment definition, preview, evaluation and use as an audience or eligibility rule            | Admin browser to CMS; campaign dispatch and loyalty read the evaluation  |
| F9  | Loyalty point redemption at checkout and at the POS                                               | Customer (bearer) or cashier to CMS; ledger debit and order discount     |
| F10 | POS service context: reservation lookup, check-in and check-out, balance collection               | Cashier browser to CMS; commerce to the Booking port                     |
| F11 | Reschedule and no-show: reference move, price difference, retention, refund override              | Scheduler or customer to CMS; Booking event to the commerce adapter      |

Trust boundaries: browser to CMS; provider to CMS; CMS to provider; module to module through ports and events only (ADR-0040 D3: Booking never imports commerce; commerce adapter lifecycle-depends on Booking events); tenant to tenant (RLS).

### F2. Deposit/payment observation and webhook replay into the payment ledger

Today's mechanism (precedent, [ADR-0017](adr/0017-external-providers-are-commerce-owned-ports-with-env-credentials-and-token-addressed-webhooks.md) D2, [ADR-0025](adr/0025-payments-are-an-allocation-ledger-separate-from-order-status.md) D6 and D7, intake in `apps/cms/src/modules/commerce/application/payment-webhook-intake.ts`, Midtrans adapter in `apps/cms/src/modules/commerce/infrastructure/midtrans-provider.ts`): a public route addressed by an opaque per-tenant token (hashed at rest, resolved through a SECURITY DEFINER function, so the tenant never comes from the payload); a neutral 404 with a latency floor for an unknown token; the provider signature verified timing-safe before any state change; a per-IP rate limit; replay as a structural no-op through a UNIQUE constraint on tenant, provider and event key; the ledger's own source key (`gateway:{provider}:{ref}`) so a re-delivery under a new event key, or the reconcile job racing the webhook, finds the leg already settled; the amount guard (`apps/cms/sql/934_awcms_commerce_payment_events_amount_mismatch.sql`); `commerce:payments:reconcile` as the independent backstop. Per O9 the platform only **observes** gateway payments and never holds or moves funds.

| STRIDE | Threat                                                                                           | Planned mitigation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Basis                   |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| S      | Forged webhook claims a deposit was paid and confirms a reservation                              | Token-addressed route plus signature verification as above. A reservation is confirmed only by the **ledger** reaching the required deposit state, observed by an event consumer; a client "I paid" claim never moves a reservation. A manual payment still needs a person's explicit accept (SECURITY.md)                                                                                                                                                                                          | Precedent               |
| S      | Tenant confusion: webhook for tenant A applied to tenant B                                       | Tenant comes from the endpoint token only; the unique key includes the tenant; composite tenant-safe references from reservation to order                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Precedent               |
| T      | **Replay**: the same webhook, or a re-sent one under a new event key, credits a deposit twice    | Event-key uniqueness plus the ledger's `gateway:{provider}:{ref}` source key; the second application is a no-op that still answers 200. The booking consumer reacting to "deposit paid" is itself idempotent (F7)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Precedent + F7          |
| T      | **Amount tampering or mismatch**: the provider-reported amount differs from the expected deposit | Today the amount guard requires the gateway `gross_amount` to equal the whole order total, and ADR-0025 D7 records that mixing a gateway leg with another tender is deferred. **A deposit is a partial payment, so this guard cannot simply be reused**: the future adapter needs a server-computed expected deposit per session, compared in integer cents, never the provider's figure, and the guard must not be loosened for ordinary orders. Needs an explicit, tested outcome table; the race is between the hold-expiry event and the payment event, which are independent                                                                                                                            | New                     |
| T      | Reconcile and webhook race produce two confirmations                                             | Both paths settle the same leg by source key; the order row is locked `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` (ADR-0025 D4)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Precedent               |
| R      | Provider or customer disputes that a payment happened                                            | Append-only allocation ledger and the stored raw-event record; payment events retained as evidence for the retention period in section 5.5; reconciliation report                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Precedent               |
| I      | Payment payloads or card data in logs                                                            | Hosted-page redirect model: no card data touches this system (PCI scope stays minimal); logs carry ids only; provider raw payloads stored in the events table, not logged                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Precedent (ADR-0017 D3) |
| D      | Webhook flooding to exhaust capacity or lock the ledger                                          | Per-IP limit (default 120 per 60 s, configurable), body size limit, work done after cheap rejection; provider outage is covered by the reconcile job                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Precedent               |
| E      | Staff types a "gateway" tender by hand to fake a deposit                                         | Gateway tenders cannot be typed by staff (ADR-0025 D7); manual tenders have their own permission and audit                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Precedent               |
| E      | Booking side stores a balance or payment status and drifts from the ledger                       | Forbidden by ADR-0040 D5 item 9: the deposit is an allocation on the linked order; reservation state stays in Booking; the reconciliation (order says paid, reservation says held) is a reported mismatch, never an automatic overwrite                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Policy                  |

### F3. Cancellation and refund coordination

Precedent: returns and refunds are additive records that compensate through the existing ledgers ([ADR-0033](adr/0033-returns-refunds-and-exchanges-are-additive-records-that-compensate-through-the-existing-ledgers.md)); the gateway refund runs in three steps with the provider call outside any transaction and the refund row id as the provider's idempotency key (`apps/cms/src/modules/commerce/application/refund-execution.ts`); an offline settlement needs its own high-risk permission and a stated reason. Gateway refunds are not auto-reversed (ADR-0025 D7). Per O9 the operator returns money; the platform books the fact and calls the provider's refund API as a merchant.

| STRIDE | Threat                                                                                                            | Planned mitigation                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Basis           |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| T      | Client chooses the refund amount or claims an earlier cancellation time to get a higher refund                    | Refund amount is computed server-side from the cancellation policy and the reservation's own timestamps (server `recorded_at`), then capped at the amount actually allocated and not yet refunded                                       | New + precedent |
| T      | Double refund: duplicate cancel event, operator double-click, retry after timeout                                 | One refund leg per (reservation cancellation); the consumer is effect-once (F7); provider call carries the refund row id as idempotency key; a `processing` leg is re-executed, a `succeeded` leg writes nothing                        | Precedent + F7  |
| T      | Cancellation succeeds in Booking but the refund event is lost (no refund) or refund succeeds without cancellation | Cancellation (Booking) and refund (commerce) are separate transactions bridged by an event; a reconciliation view lists cancelled-with-deposit and refunded-without-cancel for operator action; no distributed transaction is attempted | New             |
| R      | Operator denies having approved an offline refund                                                                 | Separate high-risk permission, mandatory reason, audit at critical level, SoD-able per tenant                                                                                                                                           | Precedent       |
| I      | Refund notices disclose amounts or booking details to a wrong recipient                                           | Notice goes to the source record's own subject; amounts are left out of messages; see F4                                                                                                                                                | New             |
| E      | Cashier cancels others' reservations to refund to a colluding customer                                            | Cancel/refund permissions separate from create; refund above a tenant threshold or offline settlement under SoD; reports of refunds per actor (monitoring)                                                                              | Precedent + new |
| D      | Provider refund API outage blocks cancellations                                                                   | The refund leg stays pending and retryable; cancellation does not wait on the provider; offline settlement path exists                                                                                                                  | Precedent       |

### F4. Reminders and notifications

Precedent: commerce-owned WhatsApp outbox via Fonnte or Meta Cloud (ADR-0017 D5); document delivery rides it (ADR-0034); campaigns carry explicit marketing consent. Upstream decided (awcms ADR-0133) on a generic `whatsapp_delivery` module with purpose classes (transactional, security, marketing), consent independent of segments, secrets cleared on terminal state, expiry for security messages, and a compatibility adapter in commerce with a written end condition. **The generic delivery module, Telegram and orchestration land upstream and arrive here by subtree sync**; until then commerce is the adapter and any new consumer calls it through a stable delivery port with no second outbox (ADR-0040 D5 item 3). Telegram is admitted by O10 (optional, off by default per tenant). Orchestration is wanted by O11 but is not admitted: it needs a written value case and an upstream ADR first.

### F5. Commission accrual events (adapter side)

The upstream commission ledger ([hr-payroll design pack, section 6](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms/blob/main/docs/awcms/hr-payroll.md)) trusts the caller's source identity and states that verifying the source is real, paid and attributable is **the adapter's duty**. The adapters live here (ADR-0040 D4): a paid order line, a completed reservation, and the refund that reverses them. Affiliate commission in `commerce` is a different thing (ADR-0040 D5 item 4).

| STRIDE | Threat                                                                                                        | Planned mitigation                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Basis               |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| S/T    | Ledger inflation: an accrual posted for an order that is unpaid, test, cancelled or belongs to another tenant | The adapter accrues only from a **paid** order line (derived from the payment ledger, not order status alone) or a **completed** reservation, in the same tenant; unverifiable sources are dropped and counted, never posted            | Upstream duty + new |
| T      | Wrong beneficiary: client-supplied staff id, or self-assignment of a sale to boost commission                 | The beneficiary comes from a server-side assignment recorded by an authorised actor (cashier on the register session, staff assigned in Booking), never from a request field; a beneficiary cannot approve their own accrual (upstream) | New + upstream      |
| T      | Duplicate accrual on event redelivery or replay                                                               | Upstream unique source identity (source type, ref, line, employment, operation) plus the registry's effect-once wrapper (F7); a different amount for the same identity is a conflict, not an overwrite                                  | Upstream + F7       |
| T      | Refund or return does not reverse the commission                                                              | The refund/return event drives a reversal accrual (new accrual, negative amount) under its own source identity; partial refunds map per line; reversal after payment is netted by the next payroll, not clawed back                     | Upstream + new      |
| R      | Dispute about why a commission was paid                                                                       | Accrual stores source ref and rule version snapshot (upstream); the adapter logs the verification result it relied on                                                                                                                   | Upstream + new      |
| I      | Events, logs or exports leak amounts, rates or staff earnings                                                 | Events carry ids and counts only; money absent from logs; visibility of earnings limited to the employee, their supervisor and HR (O8 productivity visibility principle, and the epic's ABAC scenarios)                                 | Upstream + O8       |

### 5.2 Legal role per deployment (O5)

Owner decision O5: **both, per deployment**.

- **Processor** for tenant business data: the tenant's customers and reservations, orders and payments, and the tenant's employees' workforce data. The tenant is the controller. Consequences to be confirmed by counsel and recorded per deployment: a written processing agreement with each tenant that restricts processing to the tenant's instructions; a recorded sub-processor list; assistance with data-subject requests (section 5.6); breach notification to the tenant without delay so the tenant can meet its own duties; return or deletion at termination.
- **Controller** for the operator's own platform data: staff and admin accounts of the platform, billing and contract data of tenants, operations and security logs, and abuse-control data. Consequences: the operator needs its own lawful basis, notice, retention schedule and rights-handling for these.
- **Per deployment** matters because [ADR-0024](adr/0024-awcms-one-is-template-only-derived-apps-own-their-backend.md) makes derived applications own their backend: awcms-one is a template and operates no production tenancy for a product. Each derived deployment records which role applies to which dataset in its own repository; this document supplies the matrix, not the answer for any real deployment. Where a tenant uses the operator's platform for the operator's own marketing, the roles for that dataset are different and must be separately recorded.
- Role does not change the technical controls in section 4; it changes who answers requests, who signs terms and who notifies a breach.

### 5.3 Minimisation, purpose limitation and identity

- One customer authority (commerce, O12): adapters store references, never a second copy of name, phone or e-mail. The Booking engine already stores a customer reference rather than contact data.
- Purpose is fixed per data element: the contact is for confirming and servicing the booking; geolocation (if enabled) for attendance only; the note for the operational need it was entered for. Reuse for marketing, analytics or productivity scoring needs a separate decision, a separate consent where applicable, and, for productivity, the governance review recorded in O8.
- Events, logs, audit rows and error text carry ids, never contact, note or money (F7).
- Health-revealing content (a clinic vertical's offering name or note) is high-sensitivity; default off for tenants that declare such a vertical is the upstream position and the adapter follows it (offering names in order lines and messages need a deliberate decision per vertical).

### 5.4 Attendance geolocation evidence (O6)

Owner decision O6: **geolocation only** may be used as attendance evidence. **No photo, no biometric, no device fingerprint or device identifier.** It is **off by default per tenant** and a separate, privacy-sensitive feature (epic section 8.2). The attendance engine is upstream (ahliweb/awcms#916); the points below are the constraints this repository's analysis places on it, to be carried into its design, not decisions about it.

| Dimension              | Constraint (from O6 and the epic) and open points                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Default and scope      | Off for every tenant until a tenant admin enables it; enabling is an audited, permissioned change. Applies only to check-in and check-out events; no continuous or background tracking, no location outside those events                                                                                                |
| Purpose limitation     | Purpose: verify presence at an authorised work location for the attendance record. ### 5.5 Retention

No retention period is asserted as a legal requirement here. The principle: every adapter table and queue gets a data-lifecycle descriptor from day one, with terminal-state cursors for queues and delegated purges, and a legal-hold hook; periods are set per deployment with counsel.

| Data                                              | Principle                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Payment, refund and commission ledgers; documents | Financial and tax-relevant records are append-only and retained for the period the tenant's counsel identifies; purge only through the lifecycle framework, after the period |
| Reservations                                      | Upstream retains for the life of the tenant in v1, with a per-tenant window and archive-then-purge as a recorded follow-up needing its own ADR                               |
| Notification rows (body, recipient)               | Short window after a terminal state; secrets cleared at once; recipient masked outside the single audited reveal path                                                        |
| Provider raw events                               | As long as needed for payment disputes and reconciliation; then purged                                                                                                       |
| Rate-limit counters and request logs              | Short, operational; the operator's controller data                                                                                                                           |
| Attendance geolocation                            | Section 5.4: shortest viable period                                                                                                                                          |
| Consent and suppression records                   | Kept while the suppression or consent state is relevant, because deleting them can resume unwanted messages                                                                  |

### 5.6 Data-subject rights hooks

Per-tenant answering is the upstream mechanism (every table answers the data-subject question; coverage gates `apps/cms/scripts/subject-data-coverage-check.ts` and `apps/cms/scripts/data-lifecycle-table-coverage-check.ts`). The adapters must plug into it, not build a parallel one:

- **Access and portability:** adapter tables and queues are registered for the subject-data export, with masked values where applicable; the export joins by the commerce customer reference (O12) and, for employees, the profile reference.
- **Rectification:** by appending corrected records where history matters (ledgers, attendance); in place only for plain contact data in the customer authority.
- **Erasure and anonymisation:** limited by retention duties for financial and employment records; erasing a profile or customer anonymises the identity, while ledger rows remain and resolve to the anonymised subject; notification bodies and notes are purged or nulled; a payslip or document snapshot is not rewritten.
- **Objection and withdrawal:** marketing consent withdrawal takes effect at dispatch (F4); suppression survives erasure of the message.
- **Request handling roles:** the tenant (controller) decides; the operator (processor) executes and evidences; the operator answers directly only for its own controller datasets (section 5.2).
- **Breach readiness:** audit and decision logs answer who read or changed what; notification duties and deadlines depend on the role and are to be confirmed by counsel.

## 8. Open points and what this document does not decide

1. **Deposit amount guard** (F2) is a real design gap in today's gateway flow and needs an explicit decision in the adapter ADR.
2. **Booking reference design** and the second factor for guests (F1) are proposals; the PRD and the adapter ADR decide them.
3. **Late-payment outcome table** (F2) is unwritten.
4. **Retention periods**, the lawful basis for employee data and for geolocation, the breach deadline, cross-border transfer, and every item in the "unknowns" column of section 6 are for counsel and the deployment owner, not decided here.
5. **Geolocation: raw coordinates versus verdict** is open for upstream (ahliweb/awcms#916).
6. **Orchestration (O11)** and **Telegram message classes (O10)** are gated on the upstream ADR and the upstream delivery module respectively.
7. **Stored value** (ADR-0030) is flagged for the payments assessment; its regulatory position is not analysed here.
8. This document must be revisited when the first adapter ADR is written, and again at the DoR cross-spec review (A9, [#339](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/339)). Per-domain gaps found there belong upstream.
9. **Addendum open points (F8 to F11).** Whether redemption above a tenant-set points threshold should require OTP re-verification (F9); whether check-in may be gated on the outstanding balance (F10, a PRD policy choice); and the exact numeric limits of C-26 (statement timeout, row limit, concurrency) are left to the Wave B issues and their ADRs. The step-up re-authentication mechanism named in C-40 is the platform's existing one and is not designed here. Flows F10 and F11 have no Wave B issue; their controls are requirements for the later POS service-context and booking-commerce adapter work.
