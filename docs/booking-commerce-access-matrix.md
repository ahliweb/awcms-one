# Booking-commerce adapter — RBAC / ABAC / RLS matrix

DoR artifact 5 of epic [#280](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/280), work item W6 ([#357](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/357)), tracked in [`aw-business-platform-dor.md`](aw-business-platform-dor.md). It gives the commerce-side personas of the booking-commerce adapter a minimum permission set, the attribute conditions on each grant, and the row-level-security shape of every adapter table in [`booking-commerce-adapter-data-model.md`](booking-commerce-adapter-data-model.md). It answers PRD Q10, including who may override a policy-computed refund.

> **Proposals only. Nothing here is registered or enforced.** No permission is added to `apps/cms/src/modules/commerce/module.ts`, no route checks any of it, and no table exists ([ADR-0040](adr/0040-aw-business-platform-capability-ownership-and-boundaries.md) D7). Every permission key marked **proposed (not registered)** is a candidate name; a key without that mark exists today and is cited from `apps/cms/src/modules/commerce/domain/commerce-permissions.ts`. A role name is never a permission: a tenant composes roles from permissions, so the persona columns below are suggested bundles, not seeded roles.

## 2. How a permission is built and checked today

- **Key shape.** `module.activity.action`, for example `commerce.payments.revoke`. The activity is a plural resource noun; the action is an `AccessAction` verb (`apps/cms/src/modules/identity-access/domain/access-control.ts`).
- **High-risk verbs** (`HIGH_RISK_ACTIONS`) include `delete`, `approve`, `configure`, `restore`, `cancel`, `export`, `import`, `adjust`, `reverse`, `send`, `revoke`, `manage`, `enable`, `disable`. High-risk is metadata that makes the action-time separation-of-duties check available and requires a business scope that is still resolved; it is not by itself a step-up requirement. Reads and `create`/`update` are not high-risk.
- **Precedents this matrix reuses.** Separate keys for separate authorities, never one verb stretched: `commerce.stored_value_adjustments.create` is apart from `commerce.stored_value.create`; `commerce.loyalty_adjustments.create` and `commerce.loyalty_redemptions.create` are apart from `commerce.loyalty.manage` (ADR-0026 D8); `commerce.refunds_offline.approve` is apart from `commerce.refunds.create`; `commerce.document_delivery_overrides.create` is apart from `commerce.document_deliveries.create`.
- **Step-up exists in the platform, not in commerce.** `identity-access` has an assurance level (`aal1`/`aal2`) and a server-controlled step-up freshness stamp on the session (`apps/cms/src/modules/identity-access/application/mfa-session-assurance.ts`, `domain/mfa-policy.ts` `evaluateStepUp`, route `POST /api/v1/auth/mfa/step-up`). No commerce route calls it today (verified by search). **The refund override would be the first commerce action to require it**, so wiring a fresh-step-up check into a commerce handler is new work for the adapter ADR, not a configuration.
- **Customers have no permission keys.** A shopper acts under an opaque `cs_` bearer session ([ADR-0016](adr/0016-customer-accounts-are-otp-verified-commerce-accounts-with-bearer-sessions.md) D3), never a cookie, and a `401 UNAUTHENTICATED` clears the stored session client-side. What a customer may do is decided by ownership (section 5), not by a granted key.

## 4. The matrix: persona by resource by action

Legend: **Y** allowed under the stated condition; **-** not allowed (no grant, and no route a customer can call); **own** allowed only on the actor's own rows (the ABAC condition column says how "own" is computed); **ST** step-up required; **A** audit event written. Every cell is **additionally** bounded by tenant (RLS, section 7) and by the tenant's feature flags (a booking feature that is off answers `409 FEATURE_DISABLED`, as `loyalty` and `returns` do). Permission sets are additive: a small tenant may grant one person several columns, and doing so is a grant decision recorded by the audit log, not a new persona.

Column order: Customer (C), Cashier (K), Scheduler / receptionist (S), Tenant admin (T), Finance / auditor (F).

Reading the table:

- **The scheduler sees the deposit and balance, never moves them.** A scheduler holds `commerce.payments.read` and the order read needed to show outstanding balance (PRD A4), and neither `commerce.payments.create` nor `commerce.payments.revoke`. That is the "without payment authority" requirement of PRD section 2.
- **The cashier cannot cancel, refund or override.** A cashier takes a deposit or balance and redeems points inside their own register session. A cashier holds none of `.cancel`, `commerce.booking_refund_decisions.create`, `commerce.booking_refund_overrides.approve`, `commerce.refunds.create` or `commerce.payments.revoke`. This closes the threat-model F3 elevation "cashier cancels others' reservations to refund a colluding customer": the cancel and the refund permissions are separate from create.
- **The tenant admin configures and authorises; it does not operate by default.** Cancellation-policy activation is step-up gated because a policy version silently decides every later refund amount. An admin who also runs the front desk is granted the scheduler bundle too (additive).
- **Finance reads and reconciles.** Finance reads decisions and ledgers, holds the existing refund-execution, offline-approval, reversal and loyalty-adjustment keys, and may hold the override key. It holds no reservation authority.
- **A system actor executes consumer effects.** The consumer that observes `order.paid` or a cancellation acts as the system (`actor_kind = 'system'`, the data model's decision row), under the descriptor-declared consumer mechanism (ADR-0134 in `awcms`; the four existing commerce consumers are the precedent), not under a person's permission.

## 5. ABAC conditions

Each is a condition evaluated in the handler, in addition to holding the key. "Own" is always computed on the server from the session, never from a client-supplied id (C-05).

| Persona           | Condition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | How it is computed                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Every persona     | **Own tenant.** Tenant comes from the authenticated session (staff) or from the request origin (public routes, C-01), never from a parameter. A resource of another tenant answers as not found.                                                                                                      | `app.current_tenant_id` set per request; RLS is the second line (section 7)                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Customer          | **Own reservation.** A shopper may read, reschedule-request, or cancel only a reservation whose active link points to one of the shopper's own orders; a guest uses the order's own access mechanism.                                                                                                 | `reservation_link -> order -> orders.customer_id = <bearer session's customer>`; the customer id is never an input, and no adapter table stores it (X7). Unknown, other-customer and other-tenant references answer with the same neutral 404 (C-02) |
| Customer          | **Own amount.** A customer sees the computed refund and the amount paid on their own decision, never another's; the amount is not a request field (C-10).                                                                                                                                             | Computed on the server from the policy rows and the allocation ledger                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Cashier           | **Own register session.** The cashier acts only inside an open session they hold: reservation lookup at the till, deposit or balance, point redemption. A closed session or another cashier's session is refused.                                                                                     | `register_sessions` row open and owned by the acting `tenant_user_id`, reusing `commerce.register_sessions.read/create/update`; the lookup returns only orders whose sale lines the same session may take                                            |
| Cashier           | **No reach into others' reservations.** A cashier can read a reservation to take its balance (P2) but cannot cancel, reschedule, or resolve it.                                                                                                                                                       | Absence of `.cancel`, `.update`; read limited to the order being settled                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Scheduler         | **Own tenant and business scope.** When the tenant uses business scopes, the scheduler's reservations are limited to the scope they are assigned (upstream awcms ADR-0080, scope facts, `requiredScopeType`/`requiredScopeId`); for a high-risk verb the held scope must still resolve (fail-closed). | Existing business-scope check in `access-control.ts`; adapter handlers pass the offering's scope as the required scope. Whether an offering carries a scope is a Booking-side fact (open point O-1)                                                  |
| Scheduler         | **No payment mutation.** Read-only on payments.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Key absence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Tenant admin      | **Configuration only inside the tenant**; a policy cannot be edited once active, only superseded by a new version.                                                                                                                                                                                    | Immutability trigger on active policy and window rows (data model 4.4)                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Finance / auditor | **Override ceiling.** Never above the amount paid, regardless of the actor.                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | `CHECK (final_refund BETWEEN 0 AND amount_paid)` on the decision, plus the per-payment leg cap (ADR-0033)                                                                                                                                            |
| Override actor    | **Not the requester.** A staff member who recorded or requested the decision may not also approve its override when the tenant has authored that SoD rule.                                                                                                                                            | `approve` is high-risk, so the existing action-time `checkHighRiskSoDConflicts` is available; no rule is seeded                                                                                                                                      |

Minimum ABAC test scenarios the epic's C-05 asks for, restated for this adapter (they become tests at implementation, none exists):

1. Customer A requests customer B's reservation by id: neutral 404, identical bytes to an unknown id.
2. A bearer token of tenant X used against tenant Y's origin: refused.
3. A cashier in session 1 acts on a sale of session 2: refused.
4. A scheduler without `commerce.payments.create` records a deposit: `403`.
5. A cashier calls the cancel or override route: `403`, no decision row written.
6. A caller passes `final_refund` or `amount` in a cancel request from a customer or scheduler: ignored or refused; the stored amount equals the computed one.

## 6. The refund override (PRD Q10, owner answer 10 October 2026)

**Rule.** Only a holder of `commerce.booking_refund_overrides.approve` (**proposed (not registered)**, high-risk `approve`) may set `final_refund` to anything other than the policy-computed `computed_refund`. The intended holders are a manager and a finance role; a tenant decides which of its roles carry it. Cashier and front desk (scheduler / receptionist) cannot hold it by default and no seeded bundle gives it to them.

| Requirement               | Where it is enforced                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Permission                | Handler check of `commerce.booking_refund_overrides.approve`; the ordinary `...refund_decisions.create` path cannot change the amount                                                                                                                                                           |
| Step-up re-authentication | A fresh step-up stamp on the session (aal2 and within the step-up TTL), checked in the handler through the existing `evaluateStepUp`; its acceptance time is stored as `override_stepup_at`. A stale stamp answers with a step-up challenge, never a silent allow. New for commerce (section 2) |
| Mandatory reason          | `override_reason` of 10 to 500 characters, required whenever `final_refund <> computed_refund`, rejected otherwise (data model CHECK); personal-data patterns are refused as in order notes                                                                                                     |
| Audit event               | `booking_refund_decision.overridden` at critical level: who (`override_actor_tenant_user_id`), which decision, `computed_refund` versus `final_refund`, reason code. The decision row is the durable record; the audit log is the tamper-evident one                                            |
| Never above amount paid   | `CHECK (final_refund BETWEEN 0 AND amount_paid)` at the table, the server re-computes `amount_paid` at decision time, and ADR-0033's per-payment cap still applies when legs execute, so a decision valid at the table can still be refused later if payments were partly reversed              |
| Idempotent                | One decision per reservation link (`UNIQUE (tenant_id, reservation_link_id)`) and the decision `source_key`; replaying an override finds the same row and never refunds twice (C-10)                                                                                                            |
| Not the offline refund    | Approving an offline settlement (`commerce.refunds_offline.approve`, C-11) is a different authority over a different step (how a leg is settled). The two never imply each other; a person may hold both, and a tenant SoD rule may forbid it                                                   |

An override only chooses the amount. It moves no money: refund legs still run through `commerce.returns.create` / `commerce.refunds.create` / `commerce.payments.revoke` semantics (ADR-0033) under the system actor, never under the override holder's own cash authority.

## 7. RLS policy shape per adapter table

Every table inherits the data model's generic rule (section 4 conventions there): `ENABLE` and `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, one tenant-isolation policy with `USING` and `WITH CHECK (tenant_id = current_setting('app.current_tenant_id')::uuid)`, proven under the unprivileged application role `awcms_app`, and `REVOKE DELETE` from it on history tables. **RLS provides tenant isolation only.** It does not know a role, a register session or a customer, so every persona condition in section 5 stays in the handler. That split is deliberate (the same posture as the existing commerce tables) and is why section 5 exists.

| Table                                                 | `SELECT` | `INSERT` | `UPDATE`                                                                      | `DELETE` (app role) | Notes specific to the table                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | -------- | -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `awcms_commerce_booking_offering_links`               | tenant   | tenant   | tenant (status only, by handler)                                              | revoked             | An unlink is a status change; the app role never deletes a link                                                                                                                                                      |
| `awcms_commerce_booking_reservation_links`            | tenant   | tenant   | tenant, and a trigger limits changes to `status`, `attention_*`, `updated_at` | revoked             | **No customer column.** A customer-scoped read is the join `link -> order -> customer_id` filtered in the handler by the bearer session, under the same tenant policy; RLS on `awcms_commerce_orders` is not widened |
| `awcms_commerce_product_deposit_policies`             | tenant   | tenant   | tenant                                                                        | revoked             | No tenant-default row exists, so no policy can express one                                                                                                                                                           |
| `awcms_commerce_cancellation_policies`, `..._windows` | tenant   | tenant   | tenant, frozen by trigger once the policy leaves `draft`                      | revoked             | Versions are never deleted so past decisions stay explainable; `awcms_worker` keeps `SELECT, DELETE` for the data-lifecycle engine only                                                                              |
| `awcms_commerce_booking_refund_decisions`             | tenant   | tenant   | tenant, and a trigger freezes every column except `return_id` (set once)      | revoked             | Append-only. The override columns are written only on `INSERT` of the decision, by the handler that passed section 6; no worker role needs them                                                                      |

**The customer is derived, never stored, so there is no per-customer RLS.** The "customer" condition never becomes a `customer_id = ...` predicate on an adapter table, because none carries one. A customer read is tenant RLS on the adapter table, plus the order's existing tenant RLS, plus the handler's equality of the order's customer with the bearer session's customer. A test must prove that two customers of one tenant cannot read each other's links even though RLS alone would allow it (so the handler condition is load-bearing, and the integration test must fail if it is removed).

**Roles and `FORCE`.** Because the policies are `FORCE`d, the table owner is also bound. Cross-tenant maintenance uses no `BYPASSRLS` path in app code. The `awcms_worker` role gets only the `SELECT, DELETE` the lifecycle engine needs on the history tables.

Suggested bundles (a starting point a tenant adjusts; none is seeded or registered):

| Scheduler / receptionist | `commerce.booking_reservation_links.read/create/update/cancel`, `commerce.booking_refund_decisions.read/create`, `commerce.booking_offering_links.read`, `commerce.booking_deposit_policies.read`, `commerce.booking_cancellation_policies.read`, `commerce.orders.read`, `commerce.payments.read`                                                                     |
| Tenant admin             | `commerce.booking_offering_links.read/create/update`, `commerce.booking_deposit_policies.read/update`, `commerce.booking_cancellation_policies.read/create/approve`, `commerce.settings.read/update` (feature flags), `commerce.loyalty.manage`                                                                                                                        |
| Finance / auditor        | `commerce.booking_refund_decisions.read`, `commerce.booking_reservation_links.read`, `commerce.payments.read`, `commerce.returns.read`, `commerce.refunds.read`, report keys, optionally `commerce.booking_refund_overrides.approve`, `commerce.refunds.create`, `commerce.refunds_offline.approve`, `commerce.payments.revoke`, `commerce.loyalty_adjustments.create` |

Because a small tenant may collapse bundles, the permission pairs that a tenant should be able to forbid from one subject through its separation-of-duties rules are the point of the high-risk marking:

- `commerce.booking_cancellation_policies.create` and `.approve` (author versus activate).
- `commerce.booking_reservation_links.cancel` and `commerce.booking_refund_overrides.approve` (cancel a booking versus raise its refund).
- `commerce.booking_refund_overrides.approve` and `commerce.refunds_offline.approve` (raise the amount versus settle it off-platform).
- `commerce.booking_refund_overrides.approve` and `commerce.payments.revoke` (raise the amount versus execute the reversal).

No rule is seeded: ADR-0026 and the existing refund permissions seed none either, and a default would block a one-person tenant. The matrix only guarantees that each key is separate and high-risk where it should be.

## 9. Evidence a later implementation must produce

None of the following exists. They are the tests this matrix implies, in the shape the existing commerce suites use, and a later threat-model revision may number them:

1. A registration test that every proposed key is declared in `module.ts`, enforced by at least one route, and listed in the module's permission inventory (the "permission with no enforcing code" defect class).
2. A route-by-persona authorisation table test generated from section 4 (every denied cell returns `403`; every owned cell returns `404` on another's id).
3. RLS tests under `awcms_app` for each table: a row of tenant B is invisible and unwritable with tenant A's context, the update triggers refuse the frozen columns, and `DELETE` is refused.
4. The two-customers-one-tenant integration test of section 7.
5. An override test: no permission, no step-up, stale step-up, missing reason, `final_refund` above `amount_paid`, and a replay all refuse or return the same row; success writes exactly one critical audit event.
6. A regression that a cashier and a scheduler cannot reach the override, and that holding `commerce.refunds_offline.approve` alone does not allow it.

## 10. Open points for the adapter ADR

Recorded, not decided, because each needs a code-level check that a docs-only change cannot make:

1. **O-1. Business scope on an offering.** Whether a Booking offering carries a business scope that the scheduler's assignment can be checked against, or whether the scheduler is tenant-wide in v1.
2. **O-2. Actor of a customer-initiated refund leg.** A customer's cancel executes legs that the existing returns route gates on `commerce.refunds.create` and `commerce.payments.revoke` for a staff caller. The proposal is a system actor under the decision row's authority; the adapter ADR must confirm that this does not weaken the existing routes' checks and that it appears in the audit log as a system actor.
3. **O-3. Step-up wiring.** Where the step-up check sits in a commerce handler, its TTL for this action, and the user-facing challenge (the admin UI belongs to W8, [#359](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/359)).
4. **O-4. Granularity of `commerce.booking_reservation_links.cancel`.** Whether a customer's own cancel needs a staff-visible distinction in the audit (it is recorded through `actor_kind`), and whether staff cancel is a high-risk verb for all cancels or only past a refund threshold (the threat model F3 mentions a tenant threshold).
5. **O-5. Report keys.** Section 3 cites `commerce.report_returns.read`, `commerce.report_tenders.read` and `commerce.report_loyalty.read`; whether a dedicated booking report family is wanted belongs to the metrics work, not here.
