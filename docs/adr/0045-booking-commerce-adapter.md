# ADR-0045 — Booking-commerce adapter

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 11 October 2026
- **Decision maker:** ahliweb (owner answers of 10 October 2026 applied: PRD Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q8, Q9, Q10; the owner waived PRD risk R3, check-in with a balance, so both variants are built and the owner picks the default)

## Context

Four draft documents (the ERD and data dictionary, the access matrix, the route and event contracts, and the UX flows) describe a booking-commerce adapter that does not exist yet: a thin layer in the `commerce` module that sells a Booking reservation as an ordinary commerce order. Each draft ended with open points that "a docs-only change cannot settle" and deferred them to this ADR. Waves C11 to C15 ([#378](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/378) to [#382](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/382)) cannot start a migration, a permission or an OpenAPI fragment until the answers exist, because three of them change a table CHECK, a unique index or a consumer name that is expensive to change after the first migration or the first delivery row.

This ADR decides every open point, with the options considered, and records the end of the "pending" status of the four drafts. It is **docs only** (ADR-0040 D7): it adds no migration, table, permission, OpenAPI path or code. It changes nothing in ADR-0041; where an answer touches the deposit session or the settlement event it says what [#379](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/379) owns and stops there.

The decision frame is unchanged from the drafts: Booking holds the claim on a unit of a resource and no money; commerce holds the order, the allocation ledger and the customer; the adapter holds identifiers and a lifecycle flag and nothing else (ADR-0040 D5.8 and D5.9). The tables, routes and consumers in the drafts remain proposals and take their final names at implementation, except the names this ADR freezes (D14, D15) or hands to another issue (D16).

## How to read the options tables

Every point below has a table over the seven criteria the repository uses for architecture decisions: security, performance, maintainability, scalability, compatibility, operational complexity and user experience. The cells are deliberately terse. The chosen option is in bold. A point marked **owner may revise** has a recommended default that the owner may change without reopening the rest; the cost of changing it is stated.

## Decision

### Group 1 — Link and reference shape

#### D1 — One active product per offering and one active offering per product (data model 7.6)

| Option                                                | Security                                                | Performance             | Maintainability                      | Scalability | Compatibility                                        | Ops complexity | UX                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **A. 1:1 while active (both partial unique indexes)** | Strong: an order line maps to exactly one resource kind | One indexed lookup      | Simple; reversible by an index drop  | Fine        | Matches Q1 (one nightly product per stay offering)   | Low            | Clear: one product page, one thing sold        |
| B. One product to many offerings                      | Weak: the line cannot say which resource it bought      | Extra join and a picker | Ambiguity in every consumer          | Fine        | Needs a line attribute that does not exist           | Medium         | Confusing: the shopper picks a hidden resource |
| C. Many products to one offering (rate plans)         | Neutral                                                 | Neutral                 | Needs a price-plan model Q1 rejected | Fine        | Contradicts "no computed-total line, no price table" | Medium         | Rate choice is a pricing feature, out of v1    |

**Decision: A.** Both partial unique indexes of data model 4.1 stand. A second product for the same offering, or a second offering for the same product, is refused with `409 PRODUCT_ALREADY_LINKED` or `409 OFFERING_ALREADY_LINKED`. Several room types are several products and several offerings. Relaxing the rule later is one index drop (additive); tightening it later would need a data migration, so the strict form is the safe starting point.

#### D2 — Composite keys to Booking's tables (data model 7.3)

Upstream's Booking pack states that **every reference between its tables is a composite `(tenant_id, id)` foreign key**, so each Booking table carries `UNIQUE (tenant_id, id)` by design; the migrations are not in `apps/cms` yet.

| Option                                                                                           | Security                                            | Performance         | Maintainability                       | Scalability | Compatibility                         | Ops complexity        | UX  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------- | --- |
| **A. Composite FK, with "verify against the migrated Booking schema" as a precondition of #378** | Strong: the database refuses a cross-tenant link    | Index already there | Same shape as every other commerce FK | Fine        | Follows the upstream design contract  | Low                   | n/a |
| B. Single-column FK on the globally unique `id`, plus a tenant-match trigger                     | Medium: correct only while the trigger is           | Trigger on write    | A second mechanism to maintain        | Fine        | Works if Booking omits the unique key | Medium                | n/a |
| C. No database FK, application-checked                                                           | Weak: an orphan or cross-tenant link survives a bug | Fastest write       | Silent drift                          | Fine        | Always works                          | High (reconciliation) | n/a |

**Decision: A, with a hard precondition.** Issue [#378](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/378) starts only after the subtree sync that brings Booking ([#377](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/377)) lets its author read the real constraint. If a needed `UNIQUE (tenant_id, id)` is absent, the fix is requested upstream (the Booking pack allows migration review to adjust), never worked around with B or C. Table and column names in the drafts stay provisional until then.

#### D3 — The reservation-to-order pair and the customer (X7; data model 7.7)

The pair is fixed as the drafts specify: the adapter's `awcms_commerce_booking_reservation_links` row on the commerce side, and Booking's opaque `external_ref_type = 'commerce_order'` with `external_ref = <order id>` on the Booking side, written through the Booking port. The customer is **derived through the linked order and stored nowhere in the adapter** (finding X7, owner decision O12).

| Option for Booking's `external_customer_ref`                 | Security                                       | Performance | Maintainability                | Scalability | Compatibility                                 | Ops complexity | UX                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------ | ----------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **A. Leave unset; the order is the only customer authority** | Strong: no personal data or id leaves commerce | None        | One source of truth            | Fine        | Booking says the field is optional and opaque | Low            | Booking-side lists show no customer; the adapter's reads supply it |
| B. Write the commerce customer id                            | Weak: a re-join key between two exports        | None        | Two places to erase            | Fine        | Allowed by the field                          | Medium         | Booking staff can search by customer                               |
| C. Write a per-tenant keyed hash (pseudonym)                 | Medium: not directly re-joinable, key custody  | HMAC cost   | Key rotation and erasure logic | Fine        | Allowed                                       | High           | Same as B                                                          |

**Decision: A.** The adapter never writes `external_customer_ref`. Revisit only if Booking's own reports prove to need a customer key; then C, never B, and through an ADR.

### Group 2 — Money

#### D4 — The vehicle for cancellation refund legs (data model 7.1; contracts 7.1)

Today `awcms_commerce_refunds.return_id` is `NOT NULL` and a return's `kind` is `CHECK IN ('return','exchange')` (`sql/994`); a return means "goods accepted back" (ADR-0033).

| Option                                                               | Security                                                      | Performance | Maintainability                                               | Scalability | Compatibility                               | Ops complexity              | UX                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| A. A `return` of kind `return` whose single line is the service line | Neutral; but a goods guard or restock path could fire wrongly | None        | Semantic stretch; returns reports polluted with cancellations | Fine        | No schema change                            | Low now, confusing later    | A cancellation appears as "goods returned" |
| **B. A new returns `kind = 'cancellation'` (widen the CHECK)**       | Strong: guards and reports key on an explicit kind            | None        | Explicit; one CHECK and its tests                             | Fine        | Additive; existing kinds and rows untouched | One small migration, tested | Cancellations are filterable and labelled  |
| C. Make `refunds.return_id` nullable and refund without a return     | Weak: loosens an invariant every refund path relies on        | None        | Breaks ADR-0033's "every refund has a return"                 | Fine        | Touches every refund consumer and report    | High                        | Mixed refund types                         |

**Decision: B.** The decision table's `return_id` points at a return of `kind = 'cancellation'` with one line on the service order item, `goods_gross` equal to the decision's `final_refund` (so `refund_total` equals `final_refund`: no shipping share and no separate tax refund), and **no restock path** (a service line has no stock; the new kind makes that explicit instead of relying on a service product having none). `refund_total` keeps ADR-0033's formula. The kind CHECK, the `exchange_order_id` CHECK interplay and the tests that enumerate kinds (for example `apps/cms/tests/commerce-returns-routes.test.ts`) are updated in the adapter's migration issue ([#378](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/378) or [#381](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/381)); the migration number follows ADR-0037 (the next free commerce number, not allocated here).

#### D5 — `allow_dp` and the deposit policy row (data model 7.2)

`products.allow_dp` exists (`sql/904`) and is carried and returned by the product API, but no order path reads it as a rule today.

| Option                                                                                                  | Security                                           | Performance | Maintainability                           | Scalability | Compatibility                             | Ops complexity | UX                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **A. The policy row is the only authority; `allow_dp` becomes a mirror kept in step by the same write** | Strong: one source for the threshold (ADR-0041 D4) | None        | One truth; the legacy flag stays readable | Fine        | Existing readers of `allow_dp` still work | Low            | One "Deposit" fieldset on the product form |
| B. `allow_dp` is the switch and the policy row the amount                                               | Medium: two facts can disagree at order time       | None        | Needs a consistency check on every order  | Fine        | No API change                             | Medium         | Two controls for one idea                  |
| C. Drop `allow_dp`                                                                                      | Strong                                             | None        | Cleanest                                  | Fine        | Breaking: API, import/export, tests       | High           | n/a                                        |

**Decision: A.** The order path reads **only** the policy row (a product without a row is paid in full, with no tenant default, per Q2). The product admin write path sets `allow_dp = true` when it creates or keeps a policy row and `false` when it removes one, in one transaction; `allow_dp` is not separately editable once the booking adapter feature is enabled. The data model's "configuration error to be refused" sentence is replaced: the two cannot disagree after this write path exists, and a legacy row that does disagree is corrected by a one-time backfill in the migration, not by a runtime check. The admin field lives in the product form's existing pricing area, as one "Deposit" fieldset, in [#378](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/378)'s screen work.

#### D6 — The refund when no cancellation policy applies (**owner may revise**)

The data model resolves product policy, then the tenant default, then none, and answers `0` for none.

| Option                                                                      | Security                                                | Performance | Maintainability  | Scalability | Compatibility              | Ops complexity | UX                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ---------------- | ----------- | -------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. Refund `0`, `policy_source = 'none'`, warn at setup and in the quote** | Strong: fails closed on money; an override can raise it | None        | Trivial          | Fine        | Matches the data model     | Low            | Harsh if unnoticed, mitigated by the setup warning and the quote shown before confirming |
| B. Refund 100 percent                                                       | Weak: a forgotten policy silently refunds everything    | None        | Trivial          | Fine        | Contradicts the data model | Low            | Friendly, but a loss the tenant did not choose                                           |
| C. Refuse the cancel until a policy exists                                  | Strong                                                  | None        | Extra error path | Fine        | New refusal code           | Medium         | A guest cannot cancel; support burden                                                    |

**Decision: A.** Linking an offering to a product (the active link) shows a non-blocking warning when neither a product policy nor a tenant default is active ("cancellations will refund nothing"), and the cancellation quote always states the amount and `policy_source` before the shopper confirms (`QUOTE_CHANGED` protects the figure). **Owner may revise:** changing to B or C is a change to this decision's rule and one test, not to the schema.

#### D7 — The reschedule price difference (data model 7.4; contracts 7.4; **owner may revise**)

Per Q4 the commerce order path prices a difference. The paid order, its lines and its allocations are never edited (control C-38), so a new line on a paid order is not an option.

| Option                                                                                                                                        | Security                                                       | Performance   | Maintainability                                   | Scalability | Compatibility                              | Ops complexity                     | UX                                                          |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| A. A new line on the existing order                                                                                                           | Weak: edits paid history (C-38 violated)                       | None          | Reopens a settled order and its release threshold | Fine        | Breaks ADR-0025 immutability               | High (reconcile paid versus total) | One order, one bill                                         |
| **B. A supplementary order for a positive difference, priced by the server; v1 admits only a reschedule of equal duration (difference zero)** | Strong: new money is a new order; paid history untouched       | None for zero | Reuses the order, deposit and ledger paths        | Fine        | Needs a second link role later (see below) | Low in v1                          | Same-length moves are instant; extra nights are a follow-up |
| C. Cancel and rebook as one action with a policy-free refund                                                                                  | Medium: needs a "no policy" cancellation path that bypasses D6 | None          | A second cancellation semantics                   | Fine        | Moves money for a mere date change         | Medium                             | Guest sees a refund and a new charge                        |

**Decision: B.** In v1 a reschedule keeps the stay length (the quantity of the nightly product, or one slot): the new link supersedes the old one, `priceDifference.amount` is zero and `vehicle` is null, no order is created and no ledger leg is written. A request that changes the length is refused with `409 INVALID_STATE` and a reason code, and the staff or shopper path is cancel-and-rebook under the cancellation policy. The vehicle for a later non-zero difference is fixed now so the contract does not change: a **supplementary order** (`priceDifference.vehicle = 'supplementary_order'`) priced from the original line's snapshotted unit price times the extra quantity, never from the current product price, with the deposit rule off and full payment due; a **reduction** in length is deferred and, when built, is a partial cancellation-kind refund under D4. Building it needs a `role` (`primary`, `supplement`) on the reservation link so one reservation can have one primary link and its supplements; that data-model change is made by the follow-up, not now. **Owner may revise:** if the owner wants non-zero differences in the first release, the follow-up becomes part of [#382](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/382). Owner: ahliweb.

#### D8 — Override timing (contracts 7.2)

The refund decision table is append-only: a trigger freezes every column except `return_id`.

| Option                                                                                                     | Security                                                                            | Performance     | Maintainability                                      | Scalability | Compatibility                                    | Ops complexity | UX                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A. A standalone override route after the decision is written                                               | Weak: needs an UPDATE on a frozen table or a second row                             | None            | `DECISION_ALREADY_EXECUTED` race and a state machine | Fine        | Contradicts append-only                          | High           | Two steps for the manager                                              |
| **B. Legs planned inside the cancel transaction; the override is an inline parameter of the staff cancel** | Strong: one decision, one amount, one audit event; step-up checked before any write | One transaction | One path; `DECISION_ALREADY_EXECUTED` disappears     | Fine        | Matches the append-only table and the source key | Low            | A manager performs the cancel with the override; a receptionist cannot |
| C. Both: inline plus a pre-execution amend                                                                 | Medium: an amend window is an extra attack surface                                  | None            | Two paths to test                                    | Fine        | Needs a mutable decision                         | Medium         | Most flexible                                                          |

**Decision: B.** The standalone override route of contracts 3.6 is removed. A staff cancel carries an optional `override` object (final amount, reason) that is honoured only for a holder of `commerce.booking_refund_overrides.approve` after a fresh step-up (D10). The decision row and its refund legs are created in the same transaction, with no provider call inside it (ADR-0017). The code `DECISION_ALREADY_EXECUTED` is not introduced. A later goodwill refund after the cancel goes through the ordinary refund path and its own permission (`commerce.refunds.create`), outside this decision.

### Group 3 — Actors and access

#### D9 — Who runs the refund legs of a customer-initiated cancel (access matrix O-2)

| Option                                                                                                                                                                                            | Security                                                                                                | Performance | Maintainability                      | Scalability | Compatibility                                | Ops complexity | UX                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **A. An internal application service under a system actor, entered only from the adapter's cancel path after ownership and quote checks; it plans legs and never approves an offline settlement** | Strong: no customer or staff permission is borrowed; amount bounded by `final_refund` and ADR-0033 caps | None        | One service, not a copy of the route | Fine        | Existing routes keep their permission checks | Low            | Self-service cancel works             |
| B. Execute the legs as a staff user (a service account with staff permissions)                                                                                                                    | Weak: a standing high-privilege identity                                                                | None        | Hides who acted                      | Fine        | Needs a seeded account per tenant            | High           | Same                                  |
| C. The customer cancel only files a request; staff run the legs                                                                                                                                   | Strong                                                                                                  | None        | Two steps                            | Fine        | Needs a request queue                        | High           | No self-service refund; PRD A5 weaker |

**Decision: A.** The route handlers of `commerce.returns.create`, `commerce.refunds.create` and `commerce.payments.revoke` are **not** called and not weakened; the adapter calls a shared internal service that those handlers also use after their own permission checks. The audit entry records `actor_kind = 'system'` and `initiated_by = 'customer'` (and the order, not a customer id, per X7). Legs are **planned** as pending refund legs; settling a leg off-platform still needs `commerce.refunds_offline.approve` (control C-11), which the system actor never holds. A test must prove the cancel path cannot be driven with a client-supplied amount (C-10) and that removing the ownership check fails an integration test (C-32).

#### D10 — Step-up wiring in a commerce handler (access matrix O-3; contracts 7.3)

| Option                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Security                                                                     | Performance | Maintainability                                                 | Scalability | Compatibility                          | Ops complexity | UX                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |

A handler-level check after the permission check and before the transaction, through `identity-access`'s existing step-up evaluation and its platform TTL; challenge is the existing `STEP_UP_REQUIRED`** | Strong: stale stamp refuses before any write; no commerce-owned TTL to drift | None        | Reuses one policy; one helper in `application/`                 | Fine        | Same code and shape admin already uses | Low            | The admin UI re-prompts, then retries the same request |

| C. **Decision: A.** Only the refund override requires step-up in v1 (not the ordinary cancel). The time of the accepted stamp is read from the session inside the transaction and stored as `override_stepup_at`. The implementation confirms the exact helper against `identity-access`'s current callers; this ADR fixes the placement and the absence of a commerce TTL, not the code. The admin challenge screen belongs to [#359](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/359)'s UX work.

### Group 4 — Contracts

#### D14 — Error code names (contracts 7 and section 4)

| Option                                                                                                      | Security                                 | Performance | Maintainability                        | Scalability | Compatibility                                | Ops complexity | UX                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **A. Freeze the adapter-owned set now; leave ADR-0041-domain codes to #379 and Booking's codes to Booking** | Strong: neutral, stable, no detail leaks | None        | A closed list the OpenAPI enum can pin | Fine        | Clients can code against it before the build | Low            | Stable, translatable messages |
| B. **Decision: A.** Frozen adapter-owned codes (all `409` unless stated): `OFFERING_NOT_LINKED`, `OFFERING_ALREADY_LINKED`, `PRODUCT_ALREADY_LINKED`, `PRODUCT_NOT_SERVICE`, `QUANTITY_BASIS_MISMATCH`, `QUOTE_CHANGED`, `OVERRIDE_EXCEEDS_PAID`, `BALANCE_NOT_SETTLED`. `STEP_UP_REQUIRED` (`403`) is `identity-access`'s existing code. `DECISION_ALREADY_EXECUTED` is **dropped** (D8). `POINTS_DEPOSIT_NOT_COMBINABLE` and `PURPOSE_NOT_ALLOWED` belong to the deposit-session work: [#379](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/379) fixes their names (ADR-0041 D8 says the stable code is defined in the implementation issue) and the adapter drafts then copy them. `INVALID_STATE` and `IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT` are Booking's and pass through unchanged. | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
| **A. | B. Rename at implementation                                   | Neutral  | None        | A rename after the first delivery needs a data migration                   | Fine        | Same                                                         | Higher                                                                                                      | n/a |

**Decision: A.** The names are `commerce.booking_expired_order_canceller`, `commerce.booking_cancelled_order_canceller`, `commerce.booking_cancelled_refund_coordinator`, `commerce.booking_order_paid_confirmer`, `commerce.booking_rescheduled_link_reconciler` and `commerce.booking_no_show_decision_recorder`. Commerce code never calls `applyConsumerEffectOnce` itself (`awcms` ADR-0134; the registry wraps the handler). Adding a consumer later is additive; renaming one is forbidden.

#### D16 — The settlement event name (contracts 7.6)

| Option                                                                                                                | Security | Performance | Maintainability                                          | Scalability | Compatibility                      | Ops complexity | UX  |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------- | --- |
| **A. The name and payload are fixed by #379 (ADR-0041 D7); the adapter has no consumer of it and does not define it** | Neutral  | None        | One owner for the event; no duplicate definition         | Fine        | Cannot contradict ADR-0041         | Low            | n/a |
| B. Define it here as `awcms.commerce.order.settled`                                                                   | Neutral  | None        | Splits ownership between this ADR and the implementation | Fine        | Risks a payload mismatch with #379 | Medium         | n/a |

**Decision: A.** The adapter's confirmation follows `order.paid` (the release threshold, at the deposit), and the balance a desk shows is read from the ledger (control C-36), so no adapter consumer needs the settlement event; only the loyalty earner does (ADR-0041 D7). The AsyncAPI draft's `awcms.commerce.order.settled` channel stays a placeholder that is replaced by whatever [#379](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/379) decides, and the drafts are corrected at that point.

#### D17 — Hold-to-order compensation (contracts 7.7)

| Option                                                                                                                                    | Security                                                | Performance         | Maintainability                                            | Scalability | Compatibility                                  | Ops complexity                              | UX                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. | B. **Decision: A.** No provider call and no port call enters a database transaction (ADR-0017): the hold is made first, the order and link are written in one short transaction keyed by `client_key`, and a failure after a successful hold issues a best-effort release. ### Group 5 — Re-run of control C-20 (payment boundary)

Control C-20 requires an architecture review "at each adapter ADR" that the platform stays a merchant integration with no funds holding, pooling, escrow or staff payout. Review record for this adapter, 11 October 2026, reviewer ahliweb:

| Adapter capability                          | Does it hold, pool, escrow or pay out customer funds?                                                                                                                                        | Result                            |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Deposit and balance sessions                | Charges the merchant's own order through the merchant's own gateway; the platform records a ledger fact (ADR-0041 D9)                                                                        | Pass                              |
| Refund legs and the override                | Records refund legs under ADR-0033; the override chooses an amount only and moves no money (access matrix section 6); settlement of a leg is the merchant's, through the provider or offline | Pass                              |
| Customer-initiated cancel by a system actor | Plans legs; never approves an offline settlement and never initiates a payout (D9)                                                                                                           | Pass                              |
| Rental security deposit                     | **Out of v1** (Q3; ADR-0041 D8): no "returned, never revenue" kind, no new tender, no held funds. Every deposit in this adapter is an earned part of the price                               | Pass, and the reason it stays out |
| Supplementary order for a reschedule        | A new merchant order, paid through the ordinary paths (D7)                                                                                                                                   | Pass                              |

If a rental security deposit is ever admitted, it needs its own ADR and a new legal assessment (ADR-0040 and the stored-value precedent of ADR-0030); this record does not carry over to it.

## Consequences

- Issues [#378](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/378) to [#382](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/382) have no remaining design question. Their prerequisites are: Booking in `apps/cms` ([#377](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/377)) so D2 can be checked, and the deposit sessions of [#379](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/379) so D14 and D16 can be copied.
- Four small shape changes follow from the decisions and are made in the implementation issues, not here: the returns `kind` CHECK widens to include `cancellation` (D4); the product write path keeps `allow_dp` in step with the policy row and a one-time backfill corrects legacy rows (D5); the contract drops the standalone override route and `DECISION_ALREADY_EXECUTED` (D8, D14); a commerce setting `checkin_requires_settlement` is added (D19).
- Four decisions carry a recommended default the owner may change: D6 (zero refund with no policy), D7 (equal-duration reschedule only), D12 (no refund-threshold approval) and D19 (check-in allowed with a balance). Each is changed by editing one rule or one setting, and none is a schema decision.
- The four draft documents stop being "pending": each remains a proposal (nothing is registered, migrated or served) but its open-points section now points here and marks each point resolved.
- No audit finding is closed by this ADR: control C-20 is re-run (above) and recorded; C-05, C-07 to C-11 and C-32 to C-41 stay "planned" until their implementation.

## What is not here yet

Everything the drafts propose: the tables, permissions, routes, consumers and screens. A non-zero or negative reschedule difference and the `role` column that supports supplements (D7). A refund-threshold approval for staff cancels (D12). A customer reschedule request route (D18). A branch-scoped offering (D11). A rental security deposit (Group 5). The final names of the deposit-session error codes and of the settlement event (D14, D16), which [#379](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/379) fixes.
