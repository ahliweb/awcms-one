## Context

Epic #280 proposes an "AW Business Platform" capability map: CRM, Booking, Commerce, Workforce, Notification, Analytics. It says, correctly, that the map is not a mandate for six new modules and that nothing is scaffolded before a Definition of Ready (DoR). Its reuse matrix (§2) was written on 3 October 2026. Since then the tree has moved a long way: loyalty ([ADR-0026](0026-loyalty-points-are-an-append-only-ledger.md)), the payment ledger ([ADR-0025](0025-payments-are-an-allocation-ledger-separate-from-order-status.md)), registers and cash-up, documents with the receipt/invoice boundary ([ADR-0029](0029-commerce-documents-are-separate-records-and-numbered-documents-are-immutable-order-snapshots.md)), returns, operational reports, bundles, and the upstream `inventory`, `tax` and `procurement` modules with their awcms-one adapters (ADR-0038, ADR-0039).

Two of those outcomes are a pattern worth naming. For inventory, tax and procurement, the generic capability was admitted **upstream in `ahliweb/awcms` first** (with a design pack and its own ADR), arrived here through a subtree sync, and this repository then wrote only a thin, per-tenant-moded **adapter** inside `commerce`. This ADR applies the same pattern to the epic, and re-validates the matrix against the tree as it is now. It decides placement and boundaries only. It creates no module, table or API.

## Decision

### D1 — Placement rule: three buckets, decided by who else needs the capability

| Bucket                               | Test                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Consequence                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Upstream first** (`ahliweb/awcms`) | The capability is a generic business domain with no dependency on `commerce` (it must make sense to a tenant with no store), and every AWCMS template would plausibly want it. Admission is upstream's decision under its module-admission tree. | Design pack and admission ADR upstream, module lands upstream, arrives here by a normal subtree sync (merge commit). No local divergence to speed it up.                                                                   |
| **awcms-one template**               | The work couples a generic capability to `commerce` data or flows (an adapter, an event consumer, a commerce-side projection), or extends `commerce` itself, and is reusable by several derived apps once neutral.                               | Lives here, additive inside the `commerce` module's admission discipline, only after the upstream side exists. Neutral naming; no consumer names.                                                                          |
| **Consumer repository**              | Vertical-specific semantics (a particular business's programme, session or tariff rules, branding, legal posture, scheduled jobs, production data).                                                                                              | Never here ([ADR-0024](0024-awcms-one-is-template-only-derived-apps-own-their-backend.md)). A consumer may build a minimal local abstraction first and promote the neutral part later through the explicit promotion path. |

Upstream-first is the default whenever a capability could be argued into two buckets: moving a generic capability upstream later costs a migration and a ledger rename, which is exactly what the `880`-band divergences in `AGENTS.md` are, and the reason ADR-0037 D3 widened the numbering upstream rather than here.

### D3 — Dependency classification: lifecycle versus capability, and no cycles

A **lifecycle dependency** means one context's state transitions are driven by another's events. A **capability dependency** means one context calls another's published port. Direction below is "depends on"; arrows run one way only.

- Commerce **capability-depends** on `profile_identity` (customer authority stays commerce-side for now), `inventory`, `tax`, `procurement`, `media_library` (existing).
- Booking **capability-depends** on `profile_identity` (customer reference) and has **no dependency on commerce**. A Booking reservation exposes a port and emits events; it does not import commerce.
- The **awcms-one booking-commerce adapter** (in `commerce`) is the only place that knows both: it **lifecycle-depends** on Booking events (hold, confirm, expire, cancel) and **capability-depends** on Booking's hold/quote port; Booking never calls it. Payment state stays on the commerce order; reservation state stays in Booking (epic §6.2).
- Workforce **capability-depends** on `profile_identity` and `workflow_approval` (attendance corrections, SoD). Booking reads staff availability through a Workforce **availability port**, never payroll tables (epic §8.3). Workforce **lifecycle-depends** on Booking and commerce events only through awcms-one adapters (commission accrual), so neither Booking nor Workforce imports the other.
- Payroll **lifecycle-depends** on approved commission accruals and attendance; nothing depends on Payroll except reporting.
- Notification consumers are **lifecycle-dependent** on events from every domain through `domain_event_runtime`; no domain depends on a channel.
- Reporting projections **lifecycle-depend** on every domain's events; no domain reads `reporting`.

Resulting layering (a DAG): `profile_identity` and the generic infra modules at the bottom; Booking and Workforce above them, mutually independent; `commerce` and the adapters above those; reporting and notification consumers on top. A proposed edge that closes a cycle is rejected at review, and `modules:dag:check` (already used by ADR-0039 D7) remains the mechanical guard for the module graph.

**Known mechanism gap, to be settled in Wave A item 6.** Domain-event consumers today are a static list in `apps/cms/src/modules/domain-event-runtime/infrastructure/consumer-registry.ts` that imports from `commerce` (the documented `domain_event_runtime -> commerce` exception, ADR-0026 D5). Booking and Workforce consumers cannot follow that exception: a generic upstream module must not import a consumer from this repository. A generic consumer-registration mechanism has to exist upstream before the adapters in D4 can be built. This is a precondition, not a detail.

### D5 — Non-duplication decisions (the epic's list, updated)

1. No `crm_customer` beside `awcms_commerce_customers`.
2. No `analytics` module or datastore: projections on the `reporting` engine, owned by the module whose facts they use.
3. No second e-mail or push queue, and no third WhatsApp path: new consumers (reminders, payslip notices) use the existing outboxes, with WhatsApp through a stable delivery port.
4. Affiliate commission is not employee commission; it is at most an implementation pattern.
5. `type: "service"` products are not a booking engine.
6. OMES invoice contracts are a different domain from the commerce document (see D6).
7. A vertical's booking implementation must not become the generic contract by accident; the neutral part is promoted deliberately (D1, third bucket).
8. **New:** a Booking Resource is not an inventory item and not a commerce product; link, do not merge.
9. **New:** a reservation deposit is a payment-ledger allocation on the linked order; no balance or payment status lives in Booking.
10. **New:** loyalty earn from a reservation is an event consumer writing the existing ledger under its idempotency key convention ([ADR-0026](0026-loyalty-points-are-an-append-only-ledger.md) D4); it never adds a second ledger.

### D6 — Invoice boundary: already settled; what remains

[ADR-0029](0029-commerce-documents-are-separate-records-and-numbered-documents-are-immutable-order-snapshots.md) D1 already answers Wave A item 5. A commercial receipt or invoice is a numbered, immutable snapshot of an order; it holds no balance, due date or payment state; the order and the payment ledger stay the monetary authority; an AR invoice is a different concept in its own ledger that would reference a document, and is not built. The epic's recommended direction is exactly that decision, and ADR-0035 and ADR-0039 already build on it (document money is copied from the order, tax from the snapshot).

Wave A item 5 therefore **does not need its own ADR**. What remains is small and recorded here instead:

- Booking-originated sales get their receipt or invoice from the **linked commerce order**, through the existing document lifecycle. Booking never issues a numbered document.
- The fiscal tax document (Coretax / e-Faktur and tax-invoice numbering) is out of scope for this repository, as ADR-0039 D6 already says; it is an upstream follow-up of `awcms` ADR-0127.
- A **receipt per payment** and credit notes are in ADR-0029's Deferred list. A booking deposit makes the first of these more likely to be wanted; it is a candidate for a later commerce issue, not a boundary question.
- An AR invoice stays Deferred until a finance domain exists upstream. Its own admission ADR will say how it references a document.

### D7 — The gate before Wave B to F child issues

No implementation child issue is opened, and no module, migration or OpenAPI path is added, until all of the following hold:

1. This ADR is Accepted by the owner (D1, D2 and D4 stand or are amended).
2. The upstream admission issues for `booking` and for the `hr_payroll` family exist in `ahliweb/awcms` with their design packs, following the pattern of the inventory, tax and procurement design packs under `apps/cms/docs/awcms/`.
3. The generic domain-event consumer-registration mechanism (D3) and the delivery-port question have an accepted upstream decision.
4. The ten DoR artifacts in [`docs/aw-business-platform-dor.md`](../aw-business-platform-dor.md) are each satisfied or explicitly waived by the owner, and the owner decisions listed there are answered.
5. Any OpenAPI path merged ahead of its handler is named in `ROUTE_PARITY_EXEMPTIONS` in `apps/cms/scripts/api-spec-check.ts` with the issue that lands it, and removed before its epic closes (`AGENTS.md`).

Wave B items that depend only on `commerce` (segment definitions, loyalty eligibility) may be opened earlier, once D1 and D2 are accepted and the DoR artifacts for CRM (blueprint, PRD, threat model, metric definitions) exist, because they need no upstream module. Everything that touches Booking, Workforce or notification promotion waits for item 2 or 3.

## Options considered

- **Build Booking and Workforce directly in `apps/cms/src/modules` here: rejected.** It creates a permanent local divergence on a subtree whose merge base is protected, and a generic domain would then be re-derived upstream or left to drift. ADR-0037 and the `880` band are the cost of doing this once already.
- **Put everything upstream, including the commerce adapters: rejected.** Upstream must not depend on `commerce`, and the adapters need commerce data and flows.
- **One platform ADR that also contains the Booking and HR designs: rejected.** The designs need upstream's own admission process and design packs; a template-level ADR cannot admit an upstream module.
- **Placement by upstream-first default, adapters here, consumer-specific work in consumer repos: chosen** (D1).

## Consequences

- Wave A is mostly upstream work. Of the six Wave A items in the epic, this ADR delivers item 1 and closes item 5 by reference (D6); items 2, 3, 4 and 6 are tracked in [`docs/aw-business-platform-dor.md`](../aw-business-platform-dor.md), with their target repository.
- The first real blocker is not a product decision but a mechanism: consumer registration for generic upstream modules (D3).
- Wave B CRM work can start sooner than Booking or Workforce, because it needs no upstream module.
- A consumer that needs booking before the upstream engine is released builds a minimal local abstraction in its own repository and promotes the neutral part afterwards. There is no production runtime dependency on this repository ([ADR-0024](0024-awcms-one-is-template-only-derived-apps-own-their-backend.md)).
- This ADR creates no schema, so there is no migration-band question yet; when adapters arrive they take the next free commerce number per [ADR-0037](0037-the-commerce-migration-band-is-allocated-gap-first-and-widened-upstream.md).
- The evidence column will go stale; the next ADR that changes a row should update the matrix in the same change.

## Security & privacy

- No code, data or secret is introduced. The decision constrains where sensitive domains live: payroll, attendance and employee data are upstream-owned, canonical-party-referenced, and default-deny, with the minimum ABAC scenarios of epic §12 as acceptance criteria for their admission.
- Booking must not read payroll tables; Workforce exposes an availability port instead.
- Notification of sensitive content (payslips, salary) through a fallback channel such as Telegram is out of scope unless the delivery design explicitly permits it (epic §9).
- Regulatory applicability (UU PDP, labor and payroll, payment) is a per-production-scope assessment, not something this ADR claims.
