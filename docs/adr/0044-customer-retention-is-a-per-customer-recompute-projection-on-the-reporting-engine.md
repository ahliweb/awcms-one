# ADR-0044 — Customer retention is a per-customer recompute projection on the reporting engine

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 11 October 2026
- **Decision maker:** ahliweb (owner answers of 10 October 2026 applied: metrics Q1 restatement window 35 days, Q7 guests shown as "unlinked")
- **Related:** issue [#364](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/364) (this ADR and its implementation), epic [#280](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/280) item 10, gate tracker [#339](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/339); [`docs/aw-business-platform-metrics.md`](../aw-business-platform-metrics.md) section 6 (the contract), sections 1.2, 9 and 11 (restatement, owner defaults, proof list); [ADR-0040](0040-aw-business-platform-capability-ownership-and-boundaries.md) (D5.2: one analytics store, the `reporting` engine); [ADR-0035](0035-pos-operational-reports-are-commerce-projections-over-the-existing-ledgers-on-the-reporting-engine.md) (the dimensional sink and the `security_invoker` source view this reuses); [ADR-0017](0017-external-providers-are-commerce-owned-ports-with-env-credentials-and-token-addressed-webhooks.md) D7 (the sales projections); [ADR-0025](0025-payments-are-an-allocation-ledger-separate-from-order-status.md) (the ledger that derives `payment_status`); [ADR-0003](0003-money-is-numeric-14-2-and-crosses-the-wire-as-a-string.md); [ADR-0037](0037-the-commerce-migration-band-is-allocated-gap-first-and-widened-upstream.md) (migrations `1020`–`1021`); [ADR-0024](0024-awcms-one-is-template-only-derived-apps-own-their-backend.md) (template-only: the report is generic, with no consumer name or content)

## Context

Metrics section 6 defines retention: of the customers whose first qualifying purchase fell in a calendar month (`Asia/Jakarta`), the share who made a further qualifying purchase within 90 × 24 hours of their first. Issue #364 builds the **commerce-only** half (orders; the booking input is added after Wave C) as a projection on the existing `reporting` engine, with no second analytics store (ADR-0040 D5.2), behind a per-tenant toggle that defaults OFF.

Every projection the engine carries today is **additive**: a day, a bucket and a signed delta added into a row. Retention is not. Section 6.4 says so in terms: an event can move a customer between cohorts. If a customer's first purchase is refunded in full a month later, their second purchase becomes their first and they change cohort; if a repeat order is cancelled, the customer stops being a repeater. No increment expresses either, and the spec's own late-event column requires both ("recomputing from the customer's event history, not by incrementing counters"). The decisions below answer how to hold such a figure on the engine, how to keep it deterministic, and what to do with the three facts the spec marks as mutable.

**Chosen: E.** It is the only option that is correct under late events, keeps the data inside the RLS boundary and the engine's lock, and adds no component to operate.

## Decision

### D1 — Descriptor `commerce.customer_retention`, two cursor streams, one recompute sink

One `ProjectionDescriptor` in `commerce/module.ts`, `cursor_table`, `scope: "tenant"`, `requiredPermission: commerce.report_retention.read`, the sales reports' freshness policy and `batchLimit` 500. Two streams, each with a dimensional sink and a scalar "rows consumed" counter the engine's own reconciliation can evaluate:

| Stream (`streamKey`) | Source, cursor                                                                            | Why it is needed                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `order_events`       | `awcms_commerce_order_events`, `created_at` (append-only)                                 | A transition into `paid`, a cancellation or refund out of a paid state, and the `returned` row a return announces itself with                       |
| `reversal_legs`      | `awcms_commerce_report_src_retention_reversals` (a `security_invoker` view), `settled_at` | A refund booked by hand is a settled reversal leg in the payment ledger and moves `payment_status` to `refunded` **without any order-status event** |

The view exists for the reason ADR-0035 D3 gives: the engine's cursor must be NOT NULL from insert, and a ledger leg's `settled_at` is NULL while it is pending. The view exposes only settled succeeded reversal legs and only their id, tenant, cursor, order and status.

The sink does not apply a delta. For the orders of a batch it finds the owning customers, re-reads **those customers' current paid orders**, runs the pure `deriveCustomerRetention` and upserts the customer's row, or deletes it when no order qualifies. This is idempotent and order-independent: a rebuild replays the same events and ends on exactly the rows the live pass holds, a crash-and-retry of a pass re-derives the same rows, and every customer who ever had a relevant event is recomputed at least once after their last one. Events that cannot change qualification (the creation row, a fulfilment step, a cancellation of an order that was never paid) are dropped before any query.

### D2 — The row, and what it deliberately omits

`awcms_commerce_report_retention_customers` (sql/1020) is keyed `(tenant_id, customer_id)` and holds `first_event_at`, `second_event_at` (the earliest qualifying instant **strictly after** the first, or NULL), `qualifying_order_count` and the derived `cohort_month` (the first of the `Asia/Jakarta` month of `first_event_at`, resolved through the same day resolver the sales reports use). It holds no name, phone, e-mail or order id. `customer_id` carries no foreign key, the posture ADR-0035 takes for report dimensions: a ten-year retention purge of a customer must neither be blocked by nor invalidate a historical row. "Within 90 days" is **not** stored: it is evaluated at read as `second_event_at <= first_event_at + 2160 hours` (an absolute interval, not the calendar-aware `'90 days'`), so N stays a read-time constant of v1 and the stored row does not bake it in.

The three facts the spec marks as exclusions that can change without any event — a **blocked** customer, a **purged** (soft-deleted) customer, and the **walk-in placeholder** — are not baked into the row either. The read joins `awcms_commerce_customers`, so a blocked or purged customer leaves the denominator at once and rejoins if unblocked, and a rebuild reproduces the table whatever happened to a customer's status in between ("rebuild equals live" is a statement about the table, so it must not depend on when a customer was blocked).

### D3 — Qualification, exactly

An order qualifies when `paid_at` is set, its lifecycle `status` is one of the paid states the sales reports already use (`paid`, `processing`, `shipped`, `completed`) and its cached `payment_status` is not `refunded`. ADR-0025 derives `refunded` exactly when the net settled amount fell to zero after money had come in, so a **partial** refund keeps the order qualifying and a **full** one removes it; a cancelled or expired order is not in a paid state. A second event must be a **distinct order**, so two items of one order never make a repeat, and two orders at the same instant are two orders but not a repeat (the second event must be strictly after the first). The event instant is `paid_at` (it never moves). The window is 90 × 24 hours of absolute time with the final instant inside; the cohort month is the Jakarta calendar month; a cohort is **mature** once the month's end plus 90 days has passed and is otherwise "to date".

### D4 — Small groups, "unlinked", and exclusions

A cohort under 20 customers returns its counts and a `null` percentage (`rateShown: false`); the CSV leaves the cell empty so an export cannot be used to read a rate the screen withholds. Rates are formatted from integers, round-half-up to one decimal, never from a float.

Every commerce order carries a customer id (a guest checkout creates or reuses the customer row by phone, ADR-0009), so by metrics Q7's definition — a guest **without a resolvable commerce customer id** — the one population that is "unlinked" is the **anonymous POS walk-in sale**, attributed to the tenant's shared placeholder customer. Its qualifying orders are reported once, as `unlinkedOrders` (all time), and sit in no cohort; they are not guessed into one from contact data. A guest who did give a phone is a commerce customer and is linked by that id (which is the customer authority, owner decision O12). The metrics spec's other exclusions that this repository has no concept of (test and internal accounts) are not modelled; a derived application that adds one filters it in the same read.

### D5 — Restatement: a log, written only on the live path

Metrics section 1.2 flags a period that a late event changed after the 35-day window as "restated". That is a statement about **when** a cohort changed, which a derived table cannot recover, so the sink writes one row per cohort month to `awcms_commerce_report_retention_restated` (the latest restatement instant) when a recompute changes a cohort whose month end plus 35 days has passed. The rule (`restatedCohortMonths`, pure) separates a restatement from ordinary maturation: a customer joining, leaving or moving a closed cohort restates it (in the normal flow a customer only ever joins the **current** month, so a join to a closed cohort is a backdated fact); a repeat **lost** restates; a repeat **gained** restates only once the cohort is already mature, because before maturity it is just the cohort filling in. The write is skipped while a rebuild run is `running` for the tenant and projection: a rebuild re-derives every row from nothing and would otherwise "restate" every old cohort. `resetForTenant` deletes the customer table and leaves this log alone — it is evidence, not a derivation — so the byte-for-byte rebuild comparison is over the customer table. The figure is still corrected, never frozen (section 1.2).

### D6 — Permissions, toggle, audit

One activity, `report_retention`, with `read` and the high-risk `export`: `commerce.report_retention.read` / `.export`, existing `AccessAction` verbs only. Neither is implied by `reporting.dashboard.read`, by `commerce.customers.read` (which opens individual customers) or by another report key: counting how many came back is not listing who. The tenant feature `retention` (`commerce` settings, **default OFF**) gates the surface: while off, the routes answer `200` with `enabled: false` and no cohorts (the ADR-0035 D6 rule, not `409`), the screen renders nothing and the CSV is a header row only. The projection keeps running while the toggle is off, so switching it on shows history at once. Every CSV export is audited as `retention_report.export` (the month span and row count, never a cell) and is `Cache-Control: no-store`.

### D7 — Reconcile, rebuild, export

`computeSourceTotals` walks every customer that has a paid order (keyset by customer id, paged) and sums `deriveCustomerRetention` — the same function the sink applies — into three integers (customers, repeaters, qualifying orders); `readProjectionTotals` computes the same three from the table. A mismatch while the projection is merely `delayed` is expected, as for every projection. The engine's own scalar counters are `COUNT(*)` rules over the two streams. `resetForTenant` deletes the tenant's customer rows in the rebuild's own transaction. Export (`exportRows`) writes cohort aggregates — month, customers, repeaters — and never a customer.

### D8 — Migrations and what is not here

`sql/1020` (both tables, the index, the view, worker grants) and `sql/1021` (the two permission rows), from the allocation `1020`–`1024`; `1022`–`1024` are unused. Both tables register `dataLifecycle` (cursor `cohort_month`, the 3650-day ceiling, `hard_delete`) and `subjectData` (`unreachableBySubject`, `retain_under_obligation`, the same posture as `commerce.customers`) descriptors; the worker's grants are mirrored in `WORKER_ROLE_GRANTS`.

**Not here, by name:** the **booking** input (a confirmed booking as a first or second event) is added after Wave C and will extend the recompute's facts, not its shape; the **CRM win-back list** (who did not return) is a CRM segment behind its own permissions and consent and reads this definition, never this table's rows; a **configurable N** is a v2 configuration, not a different truth; the **`profile_identity` harmonisation** of customer ids is a separate future ADR (metrics Q7).

## Consequences

- **Cost model.** A pass costs one query for the batch's owners, one for their orders and one for their stored rows, plus one upsert or delete per affected customer — bounded by `batchLimit` and by the customers' own order counts. A customer with thousands of orders is recomputed from all of them on each relevant event; that is a deliberate trade for correctness and is bounded in practice by how many orders one customer places.
- **Known limit, inherited.** The engine resumes at `cursor >= last + 1 ms` with a 500-row page, so rows sharing the last row's millisecond that did not fit the page are skipped by the incremental pass (ADR-0035 D7). Reconcile reports it and a rebuild repairs it.
- **Reads join the customer table.** The report's three exclusions are evaluated at request time; the cost is one indexed join per read.
- **A recompute reads current state.** A change with no event on either stream would not trigger a recompute. The mutable facts that matter (status, payment status) are covered by the two streams; `paid_at` and the customer reference never change.
- **Two local conventions** other commerce projections do not need: a keyed per-customer table behind a dimensional sink (the descriptor contract expresses it, so the spec's blocker clause — raise it upstream rather than build an external store — did not trigger), and a restatement log the rebuild leaves in place.

## Alternatives considered

- **Store `repeat_within_window` on the row** — rejected: it bakes N into stored state, so a future configurable window would need a rebuild for what is a read-time rule.
- **Bake the blocked/purged/walk-in exclusions into the sink** — rejected: they change with no event, so the table would depend on when they changed and rebuild would stop equalling live.
- **Detect a rebuild in the sink by a flag in the descriptor** — rejected: the engine owns rebuild state in `awcms_reporting_rebuild_runs`; the sink reads that table rather than invent a second signal.
- **Store the order ids of the first two events** — rejected: no consumer needs them, and an order id in the row is one more thing the subject-data analysis would have to answer for.
