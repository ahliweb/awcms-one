# ADR-0043 — Loyalty points are redeemed as a server-priced discount line, written in the same transaction as the ledger debit

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 11 October 2026
- **Decision maker:** ahliweb (owner answers of 10 October 2026 applied: PRD Q6 and Q8)
- **Related:** issue [#363](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/363) (this ADR and its implementation), epic [#280](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/280), gate tracker [#339](https://github.com/ahliweb/awcms-one/issues/339); [ADR-0026](0026-loyalty-points-are-an-append-only-ledger.md) (the points ledger; **this ADR closes its deferred "redemption into checkout pricing and POS tender" item**); [ADR-0025](0025-payments-are-an-allocation-ledger-separate-from-order-status.md) (the payment ledger a redemption deliberately does not enter); [ADR-0028](0028-pos-register-sessions-and-cash-up.md) (POS sales and registers); [ADR-0033](0033-returns-refunds-and-exchanges-are-additive-records-that-compensate-through-the-existing-ledgers.md) (refunds computed on cash paid; D9 earn reversal); [ADR-0041](0041-gateway-deposit-sessions-and-mixed-tenders-on-one-order.md) (D8: points and a deposit never combine); [ADR-0016](0016-customer-accounts-are-otp-verified-commerce-accounts-with-bearer-sessions.md) D3 (bearer sessions, never a cookie); [ADR-0003](0003-money-is-numeric-14-2-and-crosses-the-wire-as-a-string.md) (money); [ADR-0037](0037-the-commerce-migration-band-is-allocated-gap-first-and-widened-upstream.md) (migrations `1010`–`1014`); threat-model controls C-30 to C-33 of [`docs/aw-business-platform-threat-model.md`](../aw-business-platform-threat-model.md) (flow F9); PRD stories L3 and L4 of [`docs/aw-business-platform-prd.md`](../aw-business-platform-prd.md)

## Context

ADR-0026 built the points ledger and deliberately stopped before pricing: "redeem records the points debit only. Turning points into a discount needs #285's tender model and a decision on what a point is worth; no `redeem_value` field exists yet, rather than a placeholder number." The tender model landed (ADR-0025) and the owner has now answered what a point is worth (PRD Q6): a **per-tenant integer number of rupiah per point with no default**, whole points only, an **optional per-tenant cap** on the share of the goods subtotal payable in points, and shipping and tax never payable in points. Owner answer Q8: points and a refundable deposit cannot share an order in v1.

A point becomes money here, so the assets are ledger integrity and server-authoritative pricing. The risks threat-model flow F9 names are redemption against another customer's balance, client-supplied price tampering, redeeming against shipping or tax or beyond the cap, a double spend by replay, a race that overdraws, a negative or fractional balance, and points plus a deposit turned into a cash refund.

## Options considered

| Option | Security | Performance | Maintainability | Compatibility | Operational complexity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. A new **points tender** in the payment ledger (`tender_type = 'points'`), settled like cash or QRIS | Weak: a tender leg reverses "the way it came" (ADR-0025, ADR-0033), so a refund of a points leg would be a points movement dressed as a payment reversal; points would also enter register cash-up, the outstanding-balances report and the gateway amount guard, each a new place for a points figure to be mistaken for money | Neutral | Touches the tender CHECK, the planner, cash-up, gateway guard and every report that sums legs | Changes a table ADR-0025 froze and every payload that lists payments | A points leg needs its own reversal and reconciliation story |
| B. **A discount line on the order, written in the same transaction as the ledger debit** (chosen) | Strong: the order's `total` is simply lower; the payment ledger, cash-up and the gateway guard see an ordinary smaller order and need no change; the debit and the discount share one transaction and one account lock | One extra locked row read on an order that redeems; nothing for an order that does not | One new table, one column, one application file; the pricing function is pure and unit-tested | Orders without points are byte-identical; an order record gains two additive fields | One feature flag and one tenant setting |
| C. Convert points into **store credit** first, then tender the credit (ADR-0030) | Mixed: conversion is a second ledger movement that needs its own atomicity, expiry, and refund rules, and it turns points (not a liability, ADR-0026) into a stored-value liability the tenant never agreed to | Two ledgers per redemption | Reuses stored value but blurs two ledgers the epic keeps apart | Changes what a point is in the accounting sense | Stored-value regulation and accounting obligations arrive with every redeemed point |
| D. Keep redemption deferred; staff apply a manual discount | Weak: a free-text manual discount has no link to the debit, no cap, no idempotency and no reversal | None | Nothing to maintain | Fully compatible | Every redemption is a staff reconciliation task; defeats PRD L3 |

**Chosen: B.** It is the only option that adds no new way for a points figure to enter the money ledgers.

## Decision

### D1 — A redemption is a discount line plus a ledger debit, committed together

Spending points on an order writes, in the order's own transaction: a `redeem` row on the points ledger (ADR-0026, through `appendLedgerEntry`, the one writer), and a write-once row in the new `awcms_commerce_loyalty_redemptions` (one per order) that records the points, the rate and cap in force, the goods basis the cap was measured against and the discount. The order's `total` is already net of the discount, and the same discount sits on the order header as `awcms_commerce_orders.loyalty_discount` (`numeric(14,2)`, `>= 0`). It is **not** folded into `discount` or `voucher_discount`: those two already overlap for a voucher order (a pre-existing quirk this ADR does not touch), and a points discount must stay separately reportable and separately refundable.

Order is deliberate. A returned 4xx commits the transaction (`defineTenantRoute`), so every refusal is found **before** any row of the order exists: `prepareRedemption` resolves the terms, computes the discount, locks the customer's account row `FOR UPDATE`, lazily expires lapsed lots, and checks the balance; only then is the order inserted, and `commitRedemption` appends the debit and the redemption record. The account lock is held to the end of the transaction, so the debit cannot fail for a balance reason after the order exists. A refusal leaves behind nothing but the idempotent lazy-expiry rows the manual redeem already writes (and, on the POS, the find-or-create of the sale's customer that every earlier refusal there also leaves).

### D2 — The client sends whole points; the server prices (control C-31)

The only redemption figure a client sends is `loyaltyRedemption: { points }` on a storefront order, or on a POS sale. Any other key in that object (`discount`, `amount`, `rate`, `rupiahPerPoint`, `total`, `accountId`, `customerId`, ...) is a `400 VALIDATION_ERROR` naming the key, never silently ignored, so a client that sends a figure it should not is told so and a request that differs can never replay as another.

The server computes `discountCents = points × rupiahPerPoint × 100`, in `bigint`. A point is a whole number and the rate a whole number of rupiah, so the product is exact and there is no rounding step that could create a fraction of a rupiah; `sql/1010` states the identity again as a CHECK (`discount = points × rupiah_per_point`), so not even an application bug can record any other figure. The discount is bounded by the **goods basis** (`subtotal − voucher discount`, floored at zero) and, when the tenant set one, by `floor(goodsBasis × percent / 100)`. A request above either bound is **refused whole** with `409 LOYALTY_REDEMPTION_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` carrying `reason` (`goods` or `cap`) and the largest number of points that would fit; it is never silently clamped, because the points debited must be exactly the points the customer agreed to spend. Shipping and insurance are not in the basis, and tax is computed before points exist (D3), so all three stay payable in money.

### D3 — Points pay for goods; they do not reduce the tax

Tax stays computed on `subtotal − voucher discount`, exactly as today (flat or engine mode); points are a way of **paying** for goods, not a price reduction on them, so the tax base and the tax figure do not move. The alternative (reducing the tax base by the points discount) would change the figure the tax module snapshots and would make a redeemed order's tax depend on how the customer paid. It is the more conservative reading and the one that matches the owner's "tax is never payable by points". A tenant whose legal advice treats loyalty redemption as a price reduction would change this in one place (the basis passed to the tax call), which this ADR records as the extension point.

### D4 — The point value is a per-tenant setting with no default, and spending has its own toggle (default OFF)

`awcms_commerce_loyalty_redemption_settings` holds at most one row per tenant: `rupiah_per_point` (`1..1 000 000`) and an optional `max_goods_percent` (`1..100`). **No row means redemption is unavailable**, with no placeholder value shipped anywhere. `GET`/`PUT`/`DELETE /api/v1/commerce/loyalty/redemption-settings` read it under `commerce.loyalty.read` and change it under `commerce.loyalty.manage` (already a high-risk action: it sets what a point is worth); a change is audited with the old and new figures, and affects only orders created afterwards because each redemption row snapshots the rate and cap.

A new commerce feature toggle, **`loyaltyRedemption`, defaults OFF** and is independent of `loyalty`: a tenant that earns points still chooses separately whether they can be spent. Redemption is available only when `loyalty` is on, `loyaltyRedemption` is on and a point value is set; every other state answers the same `409 LOYALTY_REDEMPTION_UNAVAILABLE` (storefront order, POS) or `redemption: null` (the customer's loyalty read), so a prober learns nothing about which of the three it was.

### D5 — Atomicity, idempotency and serialisation (control C-30)

The ledger key is `redeem:<accountId>:<clientKey>` (ADR-0026 D4), with `clientKey` namespaced by the channel: `order:<the order's idempotency key>` for a storefront order and `pos:<the POS Idempotency-Key>` for a counter sale, so a key reused across the manual redeem route and an order can never collide. The order's own idempotency store does the replay work: the same key and request returns the same order and debits once; the same key with a different `loyaltyRedemption` (or none) is `409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT` because the points figure is part of the request hash (an order without points hashes exactly as before). After taking the account lock the order function re-reads its idempotency record, so a concurrent request with the same key that committed while this one waited replays instead of failing. A refused redemption is not recorded, so the same key succeeds after a top-up. Two parallel redemptions of one balance queue on the account row: the second sees the first's committed balance and is refused (`409 INSUFFICIENT_POINTS`, with `balance` and `requested`); the integration suite pins exactly one winner.

The lock order is account first on create (the new order row is invisible to everyone else until it commits, so nothing can wait on it) and order row then account on cancel; no cycle exists between them. A POS sale that also tenders a gift card or store credit locks the loyalty account before the stored-value account; a refund locks stored value before loyalty. The two can in principle deadlock for one customer; PostgreSQL aborts one, the request fails whole and may be retried.

### D6 — Ownership and cashier separation (control C-32)

A storefront redemption uses the account of the **verified bearer session** (ADR-0016 D3), never an id from the request; a guest, or a request whose session is absent, is refused `LOYALTY_REDEMPTION_REQUIRES_ACCOUNT`. A POS redemption uses the **customer already attached to the sale by its phone** (a walk-in sale has none and is refused `LOYALTY_REDEMPTION_REQUIRES_CUSTOMER`); the cashier needs `commerce.loyalty_redemptions.create` **in addition to** `commerce.pos.create`, checked through the same chokepoint as the existing second guard for a due sale, and that permission is separate from `commerce.loyalty_adjustments.create` (ADR-0026 D8). The manual `POST .../loyalty/accounts/{customerId}/redeem` route stays what it is: a points-only debit with no order (a reward handed over outside a sale); it is unrelated to pricing.

**Recorded gap — the "own-account guard".** The threat model asks that a cashier cannot spend their own points. `commerce.customers` carries no link to a tenant user (ADR-0016 D1), so the platform cannot tell that a customer is the cashier; the control is therefore reduced to what can be enforced (a separate permission, the actor stamped on the redemption record and the audit event, and the per-actor report), and the full guard waits for a customer-to-identity link. It is listed in "Not here yet".

### D7 — Giving points back (control C-33)

Cancelling or expiring an unpaid order, and settling a refund, give points back by a **compensating row with its own source identity**; the original `redeem` row is never edited. The ledger gains a kind, `restore` (`points > 0`, `reverses_entry_id` = the `redeem` it compensates, `source_type` `order` or `refund`), with keys `restore:order:<orderId>` and `restore:refund:<refundId>` so a replay, a re-run job or a redelivered event writes nothing twice. Both run regardless of the feature toggles: turning the feature off must not strand the points of an order that is later cancelled.

* **Cancel and expire** restore, in the **same transaction** as the status change (`transitionOrderStatus`, so the expiry job does it as `awcms_worker`), every point not yet returned. It is synchronous, unlike the earn reversal's event consumer, because the points are the customer's money and an at-least-once consumer would leave them stranded between the two.
* **Refund** restores `floor(points × cumulativeRefunded / orderTotal)` less what was already restored, from the **cash** refunded out of the **cash** paid (`orders.total` is already net of the discount), the same proportional shape ADR-0033 D9 uses for the earn. A half refund returns half the points; refunding every rupiah paid returns every point; a later cancellation returns only what is left. The refund's own money is also computed on cash paid: the returns module now counts `loyalty_discount` in the order discount it shares over the returned lines, so a partial return refunds the share of the money actually paid.
* **A restored point is a new lot** whose `expires_at` is the soonest expiry among the lots the redemption consumed (recorded on the redemption record when it was made; `NULL` when every consumed lot was permanent). Without that, redeeming lapsing points and cancelling would turn them into permanent ones. The expiry scan, its supporting index and the lot replay all treat a `restore` row like an `earn` row. The cost is a conservative choice for a redemption that spanned an expiring and a permanent lot: all of it returns with the earlier expiry. The alternative (one restore row per consumed lot) needs a per-lot identity the one-restore-per-redeem index forbids, and was not worth the schema for a rare case.

**Known limit.** An order paid entirely by points (goods covered, shipping and tax zero) has `total = 0`, so a *refund* has no cash to be proportional to and restores nothing; its *cancellation* restores everything. Refund of a fully points-paid order is not supported in v1 and is listed in "Not here yet".

### D8 — Points are not rewarded again

The earn base (ADR-0026 D3, `subtotal − discount`) now also subtracts `loyalty_discount`: spend paid for with points earns nothing, or redeeming and re-earning would mint points. A cancellation or refund that restores points does not interact with the earn reversal (`earn:order`/`reversal:*` keys are untouched).

### D9 — Points and a refundable deposit never share an order (owner answer Q8, control C-33)

Refused in both directions and at every layer: checkout refuses `loyaltyRedemption` on a `payment.method = dp` order (`409 LOYALTY_REDEMPTION_DEPOSIT_CONFLICT`, before anything is written); the storefront client refuses it locally and never sends it; and the database holds the invariant itself — `awcms_commerce_orders_loyalty_no_deposit_check` (`loyalty_discount = 0 OR dp_amount IS NULL OR dp_amount >= total`) — so a deposit order cannot be given a points discount afterwards and a points order cannot be given a deposit. ADR-0041's future per-product deposit policy lands behind the same CHECK.

### D10 — What else reads the discount

* **Documents (ADR-0029):** an issued invoice's `discount` is `discount + voucher_discount + loyalty_discount`, and the document-match trigger (`sql/980`) is replaced in `sql/1011` to compare the same sum; orders without points are unaffected.
* **Operational reports (ADR-0035):** a `restore` row reads in the existing "reversed up" loyalty bucket, so the bucket vocabulary and its table CHECK are unchanged; the loyalty summary gains a `restored` figure and `net` includes it.
* **A bill points cover whole:** a storefront order whose total is `0.00` after points (the goods with no shipping, insurance or tax left) cannot take a payment leg, so it is released as `paid` in the same transaction through the one status transition (the POS does the same for a free sale); it would otherwise sit in `pending_payment` until it expired and handed the points back.
* **Sales reports (ADR-0017 D7):** the sales projection's `discount` figure does **not** yet include the points discount (net revenue, which uses `total`, is right). Folding it in changes the netting-of-prior-returns arithmetic and is deferred.

### D11 — Permissions, audit, events, retention

No new permission key: reading and changing the point value use `commerce.loyalty.read`/`.manage`, cashier redemption `commerce.loyalty_redemptions.create`. Audit events: `commerce.loyalty.redemption_applied` (ids and figures only: no name, phone or free text), `commerce.loyalty.redemption_restored`, `commerce.loyalty.redemption_settings_updated` (warning severity, old and new figures) and `..._cleared`. Every ledger row, including `restore`, still publishes `awcms.commerce.loyalty.entry_recorded` (its `kind` enum gains `restore`). Retention: the redemption record is a `financial_tax` fiscal record (floor five, default and ceiling ten years, cursor `created_at`; its ledger FK is `ON DELETE CASCADE`); the settings table carries its own generic `dataLifecycle` descriptor on `updated_at` (same class and window), and because a point value untouched for ten years that is then purged makes redemption **unavailable** (it fails closed, never to a default), a tenant loses nothing it was already given; both are `retain_under_obligation` in `subjectData`. `awcms_app` is `REVOKE`d `UPDATE`/`DELETE` on the redemption record and a trigger rejects every `UPDATE`; `awcms_worker` gains `SELECT, DELETE` on it (`sql/1012`) so the expiry job can find the points an expired order spent, and `SELECT, DELETE` on the settings table for the retention purge only.

## Security and control mapping

This ADR **resolves** threat-model controls C-30 (D1, D5), C-31 (D2 to D4), C-32 (D6, with the recorded own-account gap) and C-33 (D7, D9). The evidence is `apps/cms/tests/integration/commerce-loyalty-redemption.integration.test.ts` (replay, key conflict, overdraw, two parallel redemptions with exactly one winner, tamper, no point value, the cap, the goods bound with shipping and tax still payable, deposit refusal both ways, cross-customer and guest refusal, cross-tenant RLS, toggle off, cancel/expire/refund restore exactly once and on replay, restore expiry, immutability, the POS path) and `apps/cms/tests/commerce-loyalty-redemption.test.ts` (pure).

## Consequences

* Positive: points become spendable without a second money ledger, a new tender or a change to the payment, cash-up or gateway paths; the pricing function is pure and exhaustively tested; orders without points are byte-identical.
* Cost: one table, one settings table, one order column, one ledger kind; `loyalty-ledger.ts` learns `restore` in its expiry scan; the returns, refund, document and earn paths each read `loyalty_discount`; the POS screen and the checkout gain one optional field.
* Risk accepted and recorded: the possible loyalty-then-stored-value deadlock (D5); the conservative restore expiry for a redemption spanning an expiring and a permanent lot (D7); the sales-report `discount` figure that omits points (D10); the unenforceable own-account guard (D6).

## Not here yet

The own-account guard (needs a customer-to-identity link); refunding a fully points-paid order (D7 known limit); folding the points discount into the sales-report `discount` figure; a booking adapter's deposit orders refusing points through the CHECK is in place but their order creation belongs to the adapter; tiers and campaign eligibility remain ADR-0026's deferred items; a storefront page for the balance and history.
